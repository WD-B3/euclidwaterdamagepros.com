Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Euclid, OH

Drying and repair for cabinets, subfloor and walls after an under-sink leak or a kitchen plumbing failure.

Call (216) 541-8541

A small leak under a kitchen sink can ruin a cabinet in days. Our dispatch line is open 24/7 for active water. If you can, shut the valves under the sink, empty the cabinet and take photos before anything is moved.

Kitchen water damage restoration in Euclid, OH is the drying and repair of cabinets, toe kicks, flooring and the walls around a kitchen after a leak. Cabinet boxes are often particleboard, which swells when wet and does not return to its shape.

Where kitchen leaks start

  • Drain traps and supply lines under the sink.
  • Faucet bases and sprayer hoses.
  • Dishwasher connections.
  • Ice maker and refrigerator lines.
  • Pipes inside nearby walls.

Appliance failures are covered on appliance leak water damage. This page focuses on cabinets, counters and the kitchen room itself.

Symptoms inside the cabinet

Warped cabinet bottoms, peeling laminate, dark stains on the back panel, a musty smell when the doors open and water rings under the sink are common signs. A drip that was slow enough to avoid notice can still damage the box over weeks.

Check the underside of the sink for corrosion and look behind the stored items for dark spots on the wall.

How the work goes

A crew extracts water, removes toe kicks and checks the wall base with a meter. Drill holes in the toe kicks let dry air move behind the cabinets. If boxes are swollen, a plan is made to remove only what is necessary.

Air movers and a dehumidifier are used until readings are normal. Flooring is handled by material. Hardwood floor water damage repair explains what happens when wood runs under the cabinets.

Scope and cost

The number of cabinets, the countertop type, the flooring and the drying days set the cost. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Matching existing cabinets can be the larger cost, so ask what the repair will look like.

For insurance, take photos of every wet cabinet, the leak source and the old parts. A sudden failure is often covered, a slow drip often is not, so ask your insurer.

Countertops, sinks and the stuff in the cabinet

Laminate and stone countertops usually survive a leak, because the surface is sealed. The board under laminate is the weak point. If it swells near the sink edge, the top may need replacing even when the surface looks fine.

Everything stored under the sink should come out and be sorted. Cardboard, sponges and paper goods that sat in water are thrown away. Plastic bottles and metal items can be cleaned and kept.

A realistic timeline

Day one is extraction, opening toe kicks and setting equipment. Days two through four are drying and daily readings. After that comes demolition of anything that did not dry, then repair. Cabinet matching and countertop orders can take longer than the drying itself, so ask about lead times early.

Standards we follow

ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, guides how we grade a kitchen leak and decide whether cabinets, toe kicks and subfloor can dry in place.

Safety in a wet kitchen

  • Unplug small appliances only if your hands and the floor are dry.
  • Turn off the dishwasher and the disposal.
  • Shut off the sink valve or the main.
  • Never use a gas range if the area around it is wet and you smell gas.

Prevent the next leak

Check under the sink twice a year. Replace corroded traps and old hoses. Know where the shutoff valves are. A small tray under the sink catches slow drips before they reach the cabinet floor.
