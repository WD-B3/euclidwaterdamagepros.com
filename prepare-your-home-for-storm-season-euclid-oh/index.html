How to Prepare Your Euclid, OH Home for Storm Season

A room-by-room checklist for gutters, drains, basements and roofs before the next heavy rain or snow squall.

Call (216) 541-8541

Most storm water damage starts with something small: a clogged gutter, a failed sump pump, a loose flashing or a downspout that dumps water beside the foundation. A weekend of checks can prevent most of it.

This checklist goes from roof to basement. It is general guidance, and a roofer, plumber or electrician should handle repairs outside your skill.

Roof and gutters

  • Clear leaves and debris from gutters and downspouts.
  • Check shingles for lifting, cracking or missing pieces.
  • Look at flashing around chimneys, skylights and vents.
  • Make sure downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation.
  • Check attic insulation and vents for stains and moisture.

Ice dams form when heat escapes through the roof and melts snow that refreezes at the edge. Good insulation and ventilation reduce the risk.

Foundation and grading

Soil should slope away from the house. Low spots near the foundation collect water that can find its way through cracks or window wells. Window wells need covers and a clear drain at the bottom.

Check the foundation for new cracks after each season. A hairline crack that grows or leaks is worth a call to a foundation specialist.

Sump pump and floor drains

  1. Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch the pump run.
  2. Check that the discharge line is clear and drains away from the house.
  3. Test the float and listen for odd noises.
  4. Consider a battery backup if the pump is your only defense.
  5. Clear the floor drain and make sure it flows.

Basement and stored items

Move valuables off the floor onto shelves. Avoid cardboard boxes in low areas. Know where your water main valve and electrical panel are, and how to shut each off safely. Our guide to basement water damage cleanup explains what happens if water arrives anyway.

Standard policies often exclude flood and backup. Ask your insurer.

After the storm

If water gets in, stay safe, shut off power to wet areas and call. The first hours matter most. See storm water damage cleanup for what a crew does, and water extraction for the first step.

Electrical and mechanical safety

Know where your electrical panel is and how to turn off power to a flooded area only when it is safe to reach. Keep appliances, the furnace and the water heater raised off the floor in basements where practical. A licensed electrician can advise on safe layouts.

Never walk through standing water if outlets or cords are submerged.

Windows, doors and small openings

Check seals and caulk around windows and doors, especially on the side that gets wind-driven rain. Look at the dryer vent, the bathroom fan exhaust and the hose bib penetrations for gaps that let water in. A tube of exterior caulk fixes many of these.

Garage doors with worn bottom seals let water run in during heavy rain. A new seal is inexpensive.

An emergency kit

  • A flashlight and spare batteries.
  • A phone charger or power bank.
  • Copies of your insurance policy and photos.
  • Gloves, boots and heavy trash bags.
  • A list of emergency phone numbers, including ours at (216) 541-8541.

Keep the kit in a spot you can reach even if the basement is wet.

Landscaping and drainage

Plants and mulch against the foundation hold moisture. Pull them back a few inches and keep soil sloping away. Extend downspouts with flexible pipes, and check that splash blocks are in place. French drains and regrading are jobs for a landscape or drainage contractor.

Also look at neighbors' runoff. A shared low area can send water to your side after heavy rain.

Know your shutoffs and your numbers

Walk through the house with your family and point to the water main, the electrical panel and the gas shutoff. Practice closing the water valve. Post a list of emergency numbers on the refrigerator, including your insurer and a restoration line.

After a major storm, phone lines get busy. Having numbers saved in your phone and written on paper avoids delay.

Protecting contents in low areas

Raise washers, dryers and storage shelves on blocks or pallets. Use sealed plastic bins instead of cardboard. Keep important papers upstairs or in a waterproof box. Small steps cut the cost of a basement water event by a surprising amount, and they make the cleanup faster.

Safety checklist before the storm

  • Charge phones and set aside a flashlight.
  • Know where the electrical panel and main water valve are.
  • Never enter a flooded basement where outlets may be live.
  • Move vehicles away from low spots.

Euclid, Ohio: what to know locally

Euclid receives lake-effect snow, and part of Euclid Creek Reservation lies inside the city. The city says its combined sewer system may overflow in rain even when a posting shows no overflow, which makes a working sump pump and a clear floor drain more important in older homes.

Neighbors in Willoughby, where the Chagrin River runs through town, should check their own conditions with the local office.
