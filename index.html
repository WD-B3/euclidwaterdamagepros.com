Understand the job

Water categories and what changes the cost

Two things shape most water damage jobs: where the water came from and how long it sat. The first table shows the three categories. The second lists what moves the price.

Water categoryTypical sourceWhat usually happens
Category 1Clean supply line, broken water heaterDry in place when caught early
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher dischargeClean, extract and dry, remove porous materials that stayed wet
Category 3Sewage, rising flood waterRemove porous materials, clean and disinfect, then dry

Categories come from ANSI/IICRC S500. Water can move to a higher category if it sits.

First hour

What to do in the first hour of a water loss

The first hour sets the cost of the whole job. Water that is stopped, removed and measured early causes less damage than water that is left to soak in. Use this short list while you wait for a crew.

  1. Stay safe and keep clear of wet areas where power is on.
  2. Close the main water valve if a pipe is the source.
  3. Call (216) 541-8541 and describe the source and the rooms.
  4. Lift small items and valuables off wet floors.
  5. Photograph the damage before anything is moved.

Houses are not the only calls. Shops, offices, churches and apartment buildings in Euclid also lose time and money when water sits. For those, the first questions are about who needs to be told, which units are affected and how to keep the business open while drying runs.

Whatever the building, we ask for the same basics: where the water came from, how long it has been running and what the floors and walls are made of. Those answers shape the equipment and the number of days. Our structural drying page explains how readings drive the plan.

Local knowledge

Water damage in Euclid, Ohio

Euclid sits on the southern shore of Lake Erie in the lake-effect Snowbelt, so winter brings heavy snow, ice dams and frozen pipes, and spring thaw sends water toward foundations. We take calls across the city, including ZIP codes 44117, 44123 and 44132.

Euclid had 26,107 housing units at the 2020 census, and about 10.5 percent were vacant. A vacant house can sit with a leak for weeks, which is why we ask neighbors and owners to call early when they see a stain or hear running water.

The City of Euclid runs its own wastewater plant, and about 12 percent of its roughly 25 miles of sewer is combined. The city also says an overflow may occur in rain even when a posting shows none. If your basement has taken on water after heavy rain, tell dispatch so we can plan for contaminated water.

For permits after a loss, ask the city Building and Housing Department at 585 E. 222nd Street. For flood coverage, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer. Our [[basement-water-damage-cleanup|basement water damage cleanup]] page covers the most common Euclid call.
